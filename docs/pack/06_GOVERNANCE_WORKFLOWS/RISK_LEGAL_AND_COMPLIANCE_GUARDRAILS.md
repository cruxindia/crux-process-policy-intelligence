# Risk, Legal and Compliance Guardrails

**Important:** This document defines an internal workflow and control design. It is not legal advice, a legal applicability opinion or a claim that CRUX is compliant with any law or regulation.

## 1. Purpose

Help process owners identify operational risks and control gaps, and help authorised specialists research whether legal/regulatory/contractual requirements may apply. The platform must preserve the difference between an AI-generated candidate and a professionally reviewed conclusion.

## 2. Risk lifecycle

1. Identify risk candidate from interview, process analysis, audit, incident, complaint or external source.
2. Record event, causes, consequences, context and affected process.
3. Identify existing controls and evidence.
4. Assess inherent risk using approved methodology.
5. Test/assess control design and operation.
6. Assess residual risk using approved methodology.
7. Define response (avoid, reduce, transfer/share, accept) and authority.
8. Assign action owner and due date.
9. Monitor status and retest.
10. Reassess on change or incident.

## 3. Risk register fields

- Unique ID and title.
- Category and subcategory.
- Affected entity, department, process, location, customer/service.
- Risk event statement.
- Causes and consequences.
- Existing controls and supporting evidence.
- Inherent likelihood and impact.
- Risk rating and methodology version.
- Control design/operating effectiveness.
- Residual likelihood/impact/rating.
- Risk owner and control owners.
- Treatment decision, action plan and due dates.
- Acceptance authority and decision rationale.
- Review date, status and history.

Risk categories may include operational, client/service, financial, fraud, information security, privacy, people, third-party, business continuity, reputation and legal/regulatory. CRUX must approve the final taxonomy.

## 4. Control record fields

- Control objective.
- Control description and type: preventive, detective or corrective.
- Process step and risk links.
- Owner, performer and reviewer.
- Frequency/trigger.
- Manual, automated or hybrid.
- System/report/evidence generated.
- Threshold or exception logic.
- Escalation path.
- Design assessment and operating test method.
- Test sample, result, exceptions, evidence and remediation.

A control described in an SOP is not proof that the control operates. Design and operating effectiveness require evidence and appropriate testing.

## 5. Legal applicability workflow

### Required context before researching

- relevant legal entity and entity type;
- activity/service and actual business model;
- geography and operating locations;
- client/customer/counterparty type;
- transaction or employee thresholds, if relevant;
- outsourcing, technology, data handling or third-party arrangements;
- relevant date/period;
- contract/client-specific obligations;
- existing licences, registrations or regulator interactions where relevant.

### Research and validation steps

1. Identify issue/question and factual context.
2. Search current primary/authoritative sources where available (Acts, rules, regulations, notifications, regulator circulars, official portals and relevant contracts).
3. Capture exact source, provision, issuing authority, publication date, effective date and retrieval date.
4. Explain candidate relevance and factual assumptions.
5. Identify conflicting, amended or superseded material.
6. Create applicability assessment with status: candidate, unknown, under review, confirmed applicable, not applicable with rationale, superseded.
7. Assign qualified Indian legal/compliance reviewer.
8. Record reviewer decision, rationale, date and supporting evidence.
9. Link confirmed requirements to policy clauses, process steps, controls and review triggers.
10. Reassess on material change or source update.

## 6. What AI must not do

- Declare CRUX legally compliant or non-compliant as a final conclusion.
- Invent statutory provisions, sections, circular numbers, thresholds or effective dates.
- Assume a generic industry law applies without checking entity/activity/geography facts.
- Treat a search result summary as authoritative text.
- Ignore amendments, exemptions, transitional provisions or client contracts.
- Draft a mandatory control as law if it is only an internal recommendation.
- Publish legal or compliance policy without qualified review and authorised approval.

## 7. Source quality expectations

Prefer official primary sources. Record secondary commentary only as explanatory support, not as the only basis for a legal conclusion. Store a copy or durable reference when lawful and appropriate; record retrieval date, source URL, version and hash where possible. Check current status immediately before approval/publishing where the requirement is time-sensitive.

## 8. Governance roles

CRUX must define who can:
- propose a legal/regulatory candidate;
- perform initial research;
- review applicability;
- approve internal compliance interpretation;
- accept residual risk;
- approve policy publication;
- monitor change in law and trigger reassessment.

A platform administrator is not automatically the legal approver. Business Excellence may coordinate but should not assume legal authority.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

- Every legal requirement record has a source and retrieval date.
- Applicability is specific to an entity/activity/context.
- AI candidate status is visually distinct from confirmed status.
- Qualified review and reviewer rationale are mandatory for confirmed applicability.
- Superseded sources are flagged.
- Risk ratings reference an approved scoring methodology.
- Risk acceptance is restricted by authority and severity.
- Policy publication is blocked when required legal review is incomplete.
